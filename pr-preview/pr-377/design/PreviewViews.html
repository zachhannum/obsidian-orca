Devices. EPUB is the fourth view. It shows the engine's EPUB in a frame that ReadiumCSS pages, and it turns a screen at a time, then to the next section. The chapter select stays in the bar and follows the section being read. A device is a named one, drawn with its body: a phone with its camera and the bar that goes home, a tablet with an even bezel, an e-reader with a matte body, a thicker chin and a screen with no colour. The list groups them as Phones, Tablets and E-readers, and the pane opens on the Kindle Paperwhite. A size is the screen in CSS px, and an e-reader's is approximate. A device larger than the pane scales down to fit, body included. A status line is under the device, as a reading app has one. It gives the title the book's own contents give the chapter, the page in that chapter, and the percentage through the book. A document the contents do not list shows no title. Inspect is off in this view.
Reader settings. Font is Publisher, Old style, Modern, Sans or Humanist. Text size is a smaller letter and a larger one with the step between them, of eight steps. It opens on the second, which is the publisher's. Line spacing is Publisher, 1.2, 1.5, 1.75 or 2. Side margins are Narrow, Normal or Wide, and so are the top and bottom, which set the text inside the screen as a reading app does. On a phone they also clear the camera and the bar that goes home. Alignment is Publisher, Start or Justify. Theme is Light, Sepia or Dark. Each starts at the publisher's, which is the author's CSS, and one moved off it overrides the CSS as a reading app does. A setting with a few choices draws them all in one colour, and marks the one in force by its raised pill alone. The reading is ReadiumCSS's, not one reading app's.
Kept. The EPUB view, the device and the settings are kept in the plugin's data, so the next preview opens as the last one was left. The pane's own state keeps the view too, as it keeps a page view. No note is written.
Opened where you are reading. The icon on a note swaps the pane to the book, turned to the page the pane is showing most of. The book pane keeps that page: a swap back to the manuscript, and a workspace reopened at startup, both come back to it. A reader who paged through the book comes back to the line that page opens at. Open preview to the right, on a chapter's own menu, splits instead, and the two panes follow each other paragraph by paragraph: scrolling the manuscript turns the book, and turning a page scrolls the manuscript. In the EPUB view the place is a screen. A swap in lands on the screen that holds the line at the top of the pane, and a swap out opens at the block the screen opens with. A switch between a page view and the EPUB view keeps the place the same way, and a linked manuscript leads the frame and follows it.
Turning by chapter. The toolbar names the chapter the span is at, and turns the book to another. Next chapter and Previous chapter are commands, so a reader can bind a key to the turn. In the EPUB view the select, the commands and a click in the navigator turn the frame to the chapter's first screen. A page no section covers, such as a blank verso, is named for the chapter that opened before it.
A click on a link. A link to a place in the book turns the book to the page of that place, in every view, and a linked manuscript follows the turn. The back and forward arrows in the pane's header walk the turns links made. An entry in the contents is a link, and so is the page number it prints. A link to a website opens it in the browser and turns no page. The pointer over a link is a hand, and a drag over a link still selects its text. In inspect mode, a click on a link pins its box and follows nothing.
Zoom. Single, spread and EPUB zoom, from 100%, which is fit, to 800%. The stepper in the bar is a minus button, the percentage and a plus button, and a click on the percentage returns to fit. The Mod key with plus zooms in, with minus zooms out and with 0 returns to fit, while the preview has focus. A pinch on a trackpad zooms about the pointer, and so does the wheel with the Mod key held. A drag with the Space key held moves a zoomed page, and with the key up a drag selects text. A zoomed page is drawn at the zoomed size, so the type stays sharp, and a selection and a link work as they do at fit. The zoom stays when the page turns, and the page it turns to shows from its top. A book opens at fit, and a pane keeps its zoom while it is open. Grid has no stepper and does not zoom.
Zoom in the EPUB view. The zoom draws the device larger, body included, as a reader holds a device closer. It lays out nothing inside the device again: the lines break where they broke at fit, and the reader is on the same screen. The text size in the reader settings is the other thing. It sets the book again inside a device that stays the size it was, so the lines and the count of screens change. The stepper, the keys, the wheel, the pinch and the drag with the Space key held act on the device as they act on a page, from inside the device too. The device is scaled and its text is drawn at the zoomed size, so the type stays sharp. A selection works as it does at fit, and the view follows no link at any zoom. The zoom stays when the screen turns. A page and a device are zoomed apart, so a switch between a page view and the EPUB view returns to fit.
Why a page-through. Drawing one page costs about 18 KiB; drawing the whole book to show one page costs 5.94 MiB, on every keystroke. The saving is not DOM nodes, it is that.
An embed and its warning. Layout opens no file. Orca resolves the embed through the vault and sends the bytes under the url the manuscript names it by, and the page draws those same bytes. An embed with no file behind it is counted on the bar, and the page is set without the image. The count opens the warnings under it, grouped by note. Each one is the engine's own line, and the place it named is a link that opens the note there beside the book. A warning in the author's CSS opens the CSS view at its line instead. Nothing but the author opens the panel, and it floats over the pages, so opening it turns no page and moves nothing. Escape shuts it, and so does a click in the pane. A click in another pane does not: an author goes to the manuscript to fix the note a warning names and comes back to the panel as they left it. A warning against matter orca generated, or against the sheet orca generated, is orca's own defect and goes to the console.
Never a stale page. An edit throws away the pages from before it, so nothing on screen is ever from the book as it used to be. A book that gets shorter takes you to its last page rather than showing nothing.
Selectable text is invisible spans over the drawn glyphs, so copy comes back in reading order and a screen reader reads a page. It arrives with the painter's overlay upstream.

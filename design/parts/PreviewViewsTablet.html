Her answer, therefore, was not propitious, at least not to Elizabeth's wishes, for she was impatient to get home. Mrs. Bennet sent them word that they could not possibly have the carriage before Tuesday; and in her postscript it was added, that if Mr. Bingley and his sister pressed them to stay longer, she could spare them very well.

Against staying longer, however, Elizabeth was positively resolved, nor did she much expect it would be asked; and fearful, on the contrary, of being considered as intruding themselves needlessly long, she urged Jane to borrow Mr. Bingley's carriage immediately.

The communication excited many professions of concern; and enough was said of wishing them to stay at least till the following day to work on Jane; and till the morrow their going was deferred.

Three views. Single turns one page, spread turns two and grid turns a screenful. The spread is the view that shows mirrored margins, and a tablet is wide enough for it in either direction.
Touch as on a phone. A swipe turns, a tap follows a link, and a long press selects. The arrows in the preview's bar stay for a pointer or a keyboard.
Split as on desktop. Open preview to the right splits the pane, and the two panes follow each other paragraph by paragraph.
EPUB has its controls in the bar. In a pane 700px wide or more the settings button, the device and the arrows sit in the bar beside Export. The count of warnings sits at the left of the controls, and a tap on it opens the warnings over the page. The settings hang under their button, as on desktop, with every row as tall as a touch. In a narrower pane they are under the page, as on a phone, and the count is an icon and a number there. The arrows turn a screen, and the EPUB view has no swipe. The line under the device gives the chapter's title, the page in that chapter and the percentage through the book. A setting with a few choices draws them all in one colour, and marks the one in force by its raised pill alone.

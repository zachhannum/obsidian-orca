In consequence of an agreement between the sisters, Elizabeth wrote the next morning to their mother, to beg that the carriage might be sent for them in the course of the day.

But Mrs. Bennet, who had calculated on her daughters remaining at Netherfield till the following Tuesday, which would exactly finish Jane's week, could not bring herself to receive them with pleasure before.

Her answer, therefore, was not propitious, at least not to Elizabeth's wishes, for she was impatient to get home. Mrs. Bennet sent them word that they could not possibly have the carriage before Tuesday.

Design
On and off. Inspect the page is the target in the header, as on desktop, and it is a command. On, it takes the accent. A tap on it again turns inspect off.
A tap is the click. A touch screen has no pointer to hover, so no box is outlined before the tap. A tap pins the box, and the sheet rises with its crumbs and the first rule that matched.
The box stays in view. When the sheet would cover the pinned box, the page moves up until the box is clear of it. A tap on another box pins that one, with the sheet still up.
Pulled up, the sheet is the whole pane: every rule by its layer, the computed values and Add a rule. A tap on the grabber switches between the two heights. Pulled down past its foot, it closes and the pin goes with it.
From the sheet to the CSS. A tap on a rule's line, on the control that wrote a rule, or on Add a rule opens the right drawer at that place, as the same click turns the panel there on desktop. The pin is kept for when the drawer shuts.
Letting go. A tap on the pinned box, a tap where there is no box, and the sheet's cross each remove the pin, as on desktop.

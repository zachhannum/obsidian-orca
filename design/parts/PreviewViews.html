Against staying longer, however, Elizabeth was positively resolved, nor did she much expect it would be asked; and fearful, on the contrary, of being considered as intruding themselves needlessly long, she urged Jane to borrow Mr. Bingley's carriage immediately.

The communication excited many professions of concern; and enough was said of wishing them to stay at least till the following day to work on Jane; and till the morrow their going was deferred.

The master of the house heard with real sorrow that they were to go so soon, and repeatedly tried to persuade Miss Bennet that it would not be safe for her, that she was not enough recovered; but Jane was firm where she felt herself to be right.

Devices. EPUB is the fourth view. It shows the engine's EPUB in a frame that ReadiumCSS pages, and it turns a screen at a time, then to the next section. A device is a named one, drawn with its body: a phone with its camera and the bar that goes home, a tablet with an even bezel, an e-reader with a matte body, a thicker chin and a screen with no colour. The list groups them as Phones, Tablets and E-readers, and the pane opens on the Kindle Paperwhite. A size is the screen in CSS px, and an e-reader's is approximate. A device larger than the pane scales down to fit, body included. The section and the screen are under the device. Inspect is off in this view.
Reader settings. Font is Publisher, Old style, Modern, Sans or Humanist. Size runs from 75% to 250% in steps of 25. Line spacing is Publisher, 1.2, 1.5, 1.75 or 2. Side margins are Narrow, Normal or Wide, and so are the top and bottom, which set the text inside the screen as a reading app does. On a phone they also clear the camera and the bar that goes home. Alignment is Publisher, Start or Justify. Theme is Light, Sepia or Dark. Each starts at the publisher's, which is the author's CSS, and one moved off it overrides the CSS as a reading app does. The reading is ReadiumCSS's, not one reading app's.
Not kept. The device and the settings last as long as the pane. Nothing keeps them: not the vault, not the plugin's data.
Opened where you are reading. The icon on a note swaps the pane to the book, turned to the page the pane is showing most of. The book pane keeps that page: a swap back to the manuscript, and a workspace reopened at startup, both come back to it. A reader who paged through the book comes back to the line that page opens at. Open preview to the right, on a chapter's own menu, splits instead, and the two panes follow each other paragraph by paragraph: scrolling the manuscript turns the book, and turning a page scrolls the manuscript.
Turning by chapter. The toolbar names the chapter the span is at, and turns the book to another. Next chapter and Previous chapter are commands, so a reader can bind a key to the turn. A page no section covers, such as a blank verso, is named for the chapter that opened before it.
A click on a link. A link to a place in the book turns the book to the page of that place, in every view, and a linked manuscript follows the turn. The back and forward arrows in the pane's header walk the turns links made. An entry in the contents is a link, and so is the page number it prints. A link to a website opens it in the browser and turns no page. The pointer over a link is a hand, and a drag over a link still selects its text. In inspect mode, a click on a link pins its box and follows nothing.
Why a page-through. Drawing one page costs about 18 KiB; drawing the whole book to show one page costs 5.94 MiB, on every keystroke. The saving is not DOM nodes, it is that.
An embed and its warning. Layout opens no file. Orca resolves the embed through the vault and sends the bytes under the url the manuscript names it by, and the page draws those same bytes. An embed with no file behind it is counted on the bar, and the page is set without the image. The count opens the warnings under it, grouped by note. Each one is the engine's own line, and the place it named is a link that opens the note there beside the book. A warning in the author's CSS opens the CSS view at its line instead. Nothing but the author opens the panel, and it floats over the pages, so opening it turns no page and moves nothing. Escape shuts it, and so does a click in the pane. A click in another pane does not: an author goes to the manuscript to fix the note a warning names and comes back to the panel as they left it. A warning against matter orca generated, or against the sheet orca generated, is orca's own defect and goes to the console.
Never a stale page. An edit throws away the pages from before it, so nothing on screen is ever from the book as it used to be. A book that gets shorter takes you to its last page rather than showing nothing.
Selectable text is invisible spans over the drawn glyphs, so copy comes back in reading order and a screen reader reads a page. It arrives with the painter's overlay upstream.

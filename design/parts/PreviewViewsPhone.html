In consequence of an agreement between the sisters, Elizabeth wrote the next morning to their mother, to beg that the carriage might be sent for them in the course of the day.

But Mrs. Bennet, who had calculated on her daughters remaining at Netherfield till the following Tuesday, which would exactly finish Jane's week, could not bring herself to receive them with pleasure before.

Her answer, therefore, was not propitious, at least not to Elizabeth's wishes, for she was impatient to get home. Mrs. Bennet sent them word that they could not possibly have the carriage before Tuesday.

Her answer, therefore, was not propitious, at least not to Elizabeth's wishes, for she was impatient to get home. Mrs. Bennet sent them word that they could not possibly have the carriage before Tuesday; and in her postscript it was added, that if Mr. Bingley and his sister pressed them to stay longer, she could spare them very well.

Against staying longer, however, Elizabeth was positively resolved, nor did she much expect it would be asked; and fearful, on the contrary, of being considered as intruding themselves needlessly long, she urged Jane to borrow Mr. Bingley's carriage immediately.

The communication excited many professions of concern; and enough was said of wishing them to stay at least till the following day to work on Jane; and till the morrow their going was deferred.

The spread fits the screen it is given. On its side, a phone shows two facing pages at the height of the pane. Upright, the same spread is drawn at the width of the screen, and each page is small. The view is the author's choice either way.
A swipe turns. A swipe to the left turns to the next page, spread or screenful, and a swipe to the right turns back. The arrows do the same, and a tap on the number takes a page to go to.
A pinch zooms. A pinch zooms single and spread about the point between the fingers, from fit to 800%, and the bar has no zoom control. On a zoomed page a drag with one finger moves the page. The page is drawn at the zoomed size, so the type stays sharp. The zoom stays when the page turns. Grid and EPUB do not zoom.
A tap follows a link. A link has no hand to show it. A tap on one turns the book to its place, and a long press selects text.
The bar holds what fits. Upright, the bar has the views, the chapter and Export, and the page number and the count of issues are under the page. On its side, the screen is short and wide, so they all sit in the bar.
The warnings open as a sheet. A tap on the count opens them in a sheet at the foot of the screen, with the count as its title. The place a warning names is a link that closes the sheet and opens the note there, in this pane. A tap on the grabber or outside the sheet closes it.
The page the pane is on is kept, as on desktop: a swap to the manuscript and back opens on it, and so does a turn of the phone. In the EPUB view the place kept is the screen.
EPUB keeps the controls in reach. Upright, the settings button, the device and the arrows are under the page, where the page views have the page number, and the bar keeps the views, the chapter and Export. On its side they are in the bar. The chapter names the section being read, and a choice turns the frame to that chapter's first screen. Upright, the count of issues is an icon and a number in a pill at the left of the controls, red if there is an error. The pill is as wide as what it holds, in the middle of a target the size of a touch. Its label says the rest. On its side it keeps its words. A tap on it opens the warnings.
The settings open as a sheet. The sheet dims nothing behind it, and the device is drawn smaller above it, so a change shows on the page as the author makes it. A tap on the grabber or outside the sheet closes it. Each row is as tall as a touch. A setting with a few choices draws them all in one colour, and marks the one in force by its raised pill alone.
The arrows turn a screen. The next arrow turns to the next screen, then the next section, and the previous arrow turns back. The EPUB view has no swipe. The device is drawn at the width of the pane, body included. The line under it gives the chapter's title, the page in that chapter and the percentage through the book. A long title ends in an ellipsis, so the line stays on one line and the page and the percentage stay whole.

In consequence of an agreement between the sisters, Elizabeth wrote the next morning to their mother, to beg that the carriage might be sent for them in the course of the day.

But Mrs. Bennet, who had calculated on her daughters remaining at Netherfield till the following Tuesday, which would exactly finish Jane's week, could not bring herself to receive them with pleasure before.

Her answer, therefore, was not propitious, at least not to Elizabeth's wishes, for she was impatient to get home. Mrs. Bennet sent them word that they could not possibly have the carriage before Tuesday.

The spread fits the screen it is given. On its side, a phone shows two facing pages at the height of the pane. Upright, the same spread is drawn at the width of the screen, and each page is small. The view is the author's choice either way.
A swipe turns. A swipe to the left turns to the next page, spread or screenful, and a swipe to the right turns back. The arrows do the same, and a tap on the number takes a page to go to.
A tap follows a link. A link has no hand to show it. A tap on one turns the book to its place, and a long press selects text.
The bar holds what fits. Upright, the bar has the views, the chapter and Export, and the page number and the count of warnings are under the page. On its side, the screen is short and wide, so they all sit in the bar.
The warnings open over the page. A tap on the count opens them above it. The place a warning names is a link that opens the note there, in this pane. A tap on the page shuts them.
The page the pane is on is kept, as on desktop: a swap to the manuscript and back opens on it, and so does a turn of the phone.

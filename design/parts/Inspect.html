A pinned box outlives an edit to the CSS, so the author sees the rule they just wrote join its list. An edit to the manuscript can remove the box, and then the pin comes off.
The engine's names. The crumbs and the rules show the selectors the engine matches. A section is named by its id, as section#chapter-twelve, in your CSS and in the design panel's. Its class is its role, and shows in faint type beside the id.
Where a rule came from. Book CSS comes first and names its line, and a click puts the cursor on it. The design panel's rules name the control that wrote them. A declaration a later rule beat is struck through, and one the engine skipped keeps the warning's squiggle.
Waiting on the engine. The box under a point, its fragments, the rules that matched it with their sheet and line, and its computed values all come from fleuron. Orca draws the answers and computes none of them. A pseudo-element answers for itself, and the engine has yet to name the element it belongs to by its id, so the crumb for that element does not pin it.

Chapter Twelve

In consequence of an agreement between the sisters, Elizabeth wrote the next morning to their mother, to beg that the carriage might be sent for them in the course of the day.

But Mrs. Bennet, who had calculated on her daughters remaining at Netherfield till the following Tuesday, which would exactly finish Jane's week, could not bring herself to receive them with pleasure before.

Her answer, therefore, was not propitious, at least not to Elizabeth's wishes, for she was impatient to get home. Mrs. Bennet sent them word that they could not possibly have the carriage before Tuesday.

The drawer opens when the author opens it. Opening a book puts its design in the drawer and leaves the drawer shut. The panel covers the page, and closing the drawer shows the change.
A number steps from two buttons. The stepper is a button at each end of the field. A tap on the number brings up the keyboard, and a value orca cannot read shows its error under the row.
A lock answers a tap. The first tap opens the card, which names the property, the value that overrides it and its line. A tap on the line turns the panel to the CSS view at that line.
The fonts orca can read. Where orca can read the system's fonts, the list holds them beside the vault's, as on desktop. Where it cannot, the list holds the face orca carries and the families in fonts/, and a vault with none is told where they go.
Both views. The panel header has the CSS icon, and the panel turns to the author's CSS as it does on desktop.
The other groups are the desktop's rows in this shape: Chapter openings, Headings, Scene breaks, Page breaks, Headers & page numbers and Fonts.

The Page group in the design panel sets the trim, the margins around the text and the bleed. The trim is the size of the page.

## Controls

| # | Control | Default | Key |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Trim | `US trade (6 × 9 in)` | `trim` |
| 2 | Margins inside | `0.75in` | `margin-inside` |
| 3 | Margins outside | `0.6in` | `margin-outside` |
| 4 | Margins top | `0.75in` | `margin-top` |
| 5 | Margins bottom | `0.75in` | `margin-bottom` |
| 6 | Bleed | `0in` | `bleed` |

## Trim

`Trim` offers these sizes by name:

| Name | Size |
| --- | --- |
| `Mass market` | 4.25 × 6.87 in |
| `Digest` | 5.5 × 8.5 in |
| `Novel` | 5.25 × 8 in |
| `US trade` | 6 × 9 in |
| `Demy` | 129 × 198 mm |
| `Royal` | 156 × 234 mm |
| `A5` | 148 × 210 mm |

A custom trim has a width and a height.

## Margins

The inside margin is the margin at the spine. The margins mirror: a left page and a right page swap the inside and the outside margin.

A number field takes a number and a unit: `pt`, `pc`, `in`, `mm`, `cm` or `em`. A number with no unit is in points.

## Bleed

The bleed is the band outside the trim that the printer cuts off. Art that runs to the edge of the page must continue to the edge of the bleed. If the art stops at the trim, a cut that drifts leaves a white line at the edge of the page.

A book with a bleed has every page larger by the bleed on each edge. The exported PDF records the trim, so a printer reads the size of the finished page from the file. The text stays where it is on the page.

`Bleed` is `0in` until you set it. A printer states the bleed that it takes. A common value is `0.125in`.

The preview shows the bleed around the page, and a dashed line shows the trim. A button in the header of the preview turns the line off and on. The export window lists each page that has art at the trim that does not reach the edge of the bleed.

## Page measurements

The `Page measurements` setting chooses the unit that the panel uses for the trim, the margins and the bleed. The values are `Inches`, `Millimeters` and `Points`.
